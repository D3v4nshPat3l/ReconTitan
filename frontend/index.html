What a scan actually looks at

Each check is deterministic Python. Findings carry the evidence they were derived from, and anything inferred rather than proven is labelled as a candidate.

Subdomain takeover

Resolves certificate-derived subdomains, matches dangling CNAMEs against known provider fingerprints, and reports only where NXDOMAIN or an unclaimed-resource signature confirms it.

JavaScript inspection

Downloads bounded same-scope assets and looks for API endpoints, risky DOM sinks, and source maps. Secrets are recorded as a short hash, never in plaintext.

Technology and CVEs

Identifies software from headers, markup, cookies, and asset paths, then matches versions to CVE records by CPE range rather than keyword text.

TLS and headers

Checks certificate validity and expiry, negotiated protocol and cipher, and the security headers a browser relies on when the server sends nothing.

Asset correlation

Hashes favicons three ways, including the Shodan-compatible MurmurHash3, to find related infrastructure elsewhere on the internet.

Reporting

Severity-ranked PDF with methodology, tool coverage, remediation, and an explicit statement of what the scan did not cover.
